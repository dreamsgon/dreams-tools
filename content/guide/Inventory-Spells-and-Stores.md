# Inventory, spells and stores

Nothing here is tied to one game. A campaign decides its own coins, carrying, categories and spell words.

## Set up the campaign: Items and spells

**Tools › Items and spells** sets up:

- **Coins** and what each is worth in the smallest one: one Coins, gold/silver/copper, credits, dollars and cents, or your own.
- How a **load** is counted: pounds, kilograms, slots, bulk, or not at all.
- **Item categories** and which columns show (weight, value, uses, worn or in hand).
- What the game calls its spells (Spells and Cast, Powers and Use, Prayers…), their groups, the details each lists (Range, Duration) and the word for ready ones (Prepared, or none).
- What players may do.

Start from **Fantasy**, **Science fiction**, **Modern day** or **Slots and one coin**, then change any word. The **item list** holds items you saved from a character to hand out again.

## Inventory tab

On a character page:

- **Money**: type `5 gp 3 sp` and choose **Add** or **Spend**; coins make change by themselves.
- **Carrying** against what the character can carry.
- **Items** in their categories. Each line has a quantity with − and +, a **shield** for worn or in hand, **uses** as pips, rolls to click, weight and value. Open a line for a note (write `[[2d6|Label]]` for a roll), a **GM note** players never get, **Give to…** another character, and **Save to the item list**.

## Spells tab

Named by the campaign. Groups spells, powers or prayers, each with details, tags, text with rolls, rolls to click, a star for ready ones, and an optional **cost**: *Cast* (or the campaign's word) spends that many from one of the character's trackers, such as spell slots or power points.

Every item and spell has an **eye**. Both tabs have their own tab visibility; the character's own player (Who can edit) always sees them on the player site.

## Stores

Choose **Store** as the entry type for a **shop**, a **container**, a **pile of loot** or the **party's stash**.

![A shop in GM view](images/shop-gm.jpg)

The **Stock** tab (Contents, Loot or Stash) lists items with category, how many (or ∞), value and, in a shop, what players pay. A line can be **hidden** (under the counter) or a **service** that is bought but not carried away.

**Shops**: open or closed with hours; players pay a % of value and the shop pays a % when it buys; a category or a character can have its own %; the shop buys only some categories or nothing; its purse can never run out; sold-out lines can be hidden; characters can **haggle** once every two hours with a roll against a target for a % off; each sale can **wait for your OK**.

**Containers**: open, closed (anyone opens), or **locked** (a key the character carries by name, or a roll against a target). For containers, loot and stashes you choose whether players take things, put things in, take everything or split the coins across the party.

Any store can be for picked characters only, or **run by a character** (a **stall**): their player stocks it from their inventory, sets prices and collects the coin. When the campaign allows (Yes or After my OK) players can create their own stalls, containers and stashes from their Inventory tab. Every change is logged on the page.

On the player site, prices and rolls are done on the server. Players never get hidden lines, GM notes, the shop's purse, other characters' rates or deals, the key's name or a roll's target, or anything inside a closed container.

> **Tutorial: open a shop**
> 1. Tools › Items and spells › start from *Fantasy*.
> 2. New entry › **Store** › Shop, name it *The Gilded Goose Outfitters*.
> 3. Stock tab: add three items with prices, mark one *under the counter*.
> 4. Settings: players pay 100% of value; the shop buys only weapons at 50%.
> 5. Set a PC's player to *Who can edit* the character, then watch them buy from their player site; with *wait for my OK* on, press **Yes** on the page.

## Sheet import and export

Sheet import fills Inventory from a list titled *Inventory* (or Equipment, Gear…), Spells from a list titled *Spells* (or Powers, Prayers…) and money from the file's `money`; export writes them back. See [Sheet import, export and Google Sheets](Sheet-Import-Export-and-Google-Sheets).
